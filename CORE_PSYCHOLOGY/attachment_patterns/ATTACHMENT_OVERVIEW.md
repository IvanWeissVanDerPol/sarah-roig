# Attachment Style: Sarah

---

## Primary Style: Anxious-Preoccupied

### Characteristics

| Feature | How It Shows |
|---------|-------------|
| **Fear of abandonment** | Hypervigilant to signs of leaving |
| **Reassurance-seeking** | Needs confirmation of love/commitment |
| **Hyperawareness** | Reads into every mood shift |
| **Gives > Receives** | Imbalanced emotional labor |
| **Difficulty with uncertainty** | Needs to know where she stands |
| **Protest behaviors** | May push to get response when anxious |

---

## How It Developed

### Likely Contributors
- Inconsistent caregiving in childhood (investigate)
- Early experiences of unpredictable love
- Reinforced by relationships with unavailable partners
- Sebas betrayal deepened abandonment fears
- NM's controlling behavior increased hypervigilance

### The Cycle
1. Meets someone → Invests fully
2. Any distance → Panic ("they're leaving")
3. Over-gives to prevent abandonment
4. Either: They leave (confirms fear) OR She exhausts herself
5. Repeat with next partner

---

## Triggers

### Activates Anxiety
- Partner being distant or busy
- Delayed responses to messages
- Changes in routine/communication patterns
- Seeing partner with other women
- Holiday season (Christmas especially)
- Ambiguity about relationship status

### Calms Anxiety
- Consistent communication
- Explicit reassurance
- Follow-through on promises
- Physical presence/touch
- Clear relationship definitions

---

## In Relationships

### With Avoidant Partners (Sebas, NM)
- **Dynamic:** Anxious-Avoidant trap
- **Result:** She pursues, they withdraw, she pursues harder
- **Outcome:** Exhaustion, abandonment, confirmation of fears

### With Secure Partners (Ivan)
- **Dynamic:** Her anxiety is met with consistency
- **Result:** She calms, feels safe, can be authentic
- **Outcome:** Genuine connection, reciprocity
- **Key Learning:** She CAN attach healthily when partner is capable

---

## Toward Earned Security

### What She's Learning
1. Not everyone leaves
2. Her intensity can be welcomed (Ivan welcomes it)
3. She can survive uncertainty
4. Healthy partners don't punish her needs

### Goals for Growth
1. **Self-soothing** — Calm anxiety without external reassurance
2. **Tolerance** — Sit with uncertainty without spiraling
3. **Discernment** — Identify secure vs. avoidant partners earlier
4. **Balance** — Give and receive equally

---

## Attachment in Action

### Anxious Response
> Partner is quiet → "They're mad at me" → Text repeatedly → Partner withdraws more → "See, they're leaving"

### Earned Secure Response (Goal)
> Partner is quiet → "They might be busy" → Wait, self-soothe → Partner responds → "See, I can handle uncertainty"

---

## Therapeutic Focus

### Short-Term
- Psychoeducation about attachment styles
- Identify triggers and responses
- Develop self-soothing strategies

### Medium-Term
- Challenge catastrophic interpretations
- Practice sitting with uncertainty
- Build evidence that she can be loved as-is

### Long-Term
- Internalize secure base
- Choose partners based on security, not intensity
- Respond to anxiety with curiosity, not panic

---

## Key Insight

> Anxious attachment isn't a flaw — it's an adaptation to inconsistent love. The goal isn't to become "not anxious" but to develop a secure base within herself AND choose partners who can meet her needs.

The Ivan relationship proves: **When she's with someone consistent, her anxiety decreases naturally.**
